About this guide

Sitting in the heart of Hartford County, Southington is the kind of place that feels familiar the moment you arrive — a town of apple orchards and ski slopes, of neighborhood diners and a green that fills up for festivals. Established in 1779 and home to roughly 43,821 residents as of the 2020 census, Southington has grown from a colonial farming settlement into one of central Connecticut's most welcoming communities.

A Town With Roots — and a Lot Going On

Southington's story stretches back more than three centuries. First settled in 1698 as part of Farmington, it spent its early years as the "South Society of Farmington" before becoming its own town on February 15, 1779.

Over the centuries it has hosted a visit from General George Washington (1770) and an encampment used by French General Rochambeau during the American Revolution — history that still echoes through the town today.

Today, Southington offers the best of both worlds: a genuine sense of community and easy access to everything Connecticut has to offer.
