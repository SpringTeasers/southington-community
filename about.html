About Southington

A colonial town with a modern heart.

Southington sits in Hartford County, Connecticut — a community of about 43,821 people (2020 census) spread across 36.6 square miles. It's the kind of town where history shows up in the street names and a strong sense of community shows up at every festival.

Three Centuries of History

Southington's roots run deep. The area was first settled in 1698 as part of Farmington, and for its first several decades it was known as the "South Society of Farmington." On February 15, 1779, it officially became the Town of Southington.

The town played small but memorable roles in early American history:

  • General George Washington visited the settlement in 1770.
  • During the American Revolutionary War, the area included an encampment site used by French General Rochambeau.

Through the 20th century — particularly from the 1960s onward — Southington grew into a thriving suburban community while preserving its New England character.

How the Town Is Governed

Southington operates under a council-manager form of government, which pairs an elected Town Council with a professional Town Manager who oversees day-to-day operations.
